Milwaukee and Tampa Bay finished the year on baseball’s biggest stage. Their ticket prices tell a different story from their crowd totals: tickets advertised for the World Series were far more expensive than regular-season seats. Here’s how the four-ticket benchmarks changed across the season.

Milwaukee faced the steeper World Series price jump

On the published get-in benchmarks, four Brewers tickets would have cost roughly $10,394, versus $3,222 for the Rays. The Milwaukee estimate equaled 160.1% of local median monthly household income; Tampa Bay’s equaled 49.4%.

The two metro areas had similar annual median household incomes in the source dataset: $77,919 in Milwaukee and $78,275 in Tampa Bay. Different ticket benchmarks are used at each stage, so this is an illustration of changing affordability, not a same-seat price index.

Sources and definitions

How we made the comparisons

The aim is to show turnout and affordability together without treating the two as proof of cause and effect.

Average crowds

For each period, we divided a team's announced home attendance by its number of home games. The postseason analysis covers 40 home games among nine clubs. These are not unique-fan counts.

Seating fill

Average attendance divided by the venue's published fixed seating capacity. The Rays expanded fixed seating for the playoffs. Standing room and other open areas can produce figures over 100%; this is not literal building occupancy.

Cost of attending

Regular-season family costs cover four average-priced tickets, selected concessions and parking. The cost burden equals that outing estimate divided by one-twelfth of the metro area's annual median household income.

Which teams are included?

All 12 clubs that reached the 2026 playoffs appear in the table. The Cubs, Red Sox and Phillies did not host a postseason game, so their playoff attendance figures are shown as unavailable, not zero.

World Series ticket estimates

We compared four tickets at different advertised price benchmarks: regular-season average prices, a pre-playoff resale asking-price snapshot and the average published October 20–21 World Series get-in asking prices. The income share uses one month of metro median household income (annual estimate divided by 12). Sources and fee treatment differ across stages. Results are illustrative price benchmarks, not verified purchases or like-for-like ticket comparisons.
